# Observational Editorial Balance

Use this as the default visual-fidelity band for every class: halfway between a literal photograph and an abstract symbol sheet.

## Balance

- Retain the source's full subject relationship and 4–8 recognisable anchors: bowl rim and ingredient clusters; glass rim and liquid band; facial planes and garment silhouette; product seams and key controls; horizon, ridge, or architecture profile.
- Build these anchors from 3–6 connected hand-printed colour masses plus 6–12 selective descriptive marks. Let at least half of the visual information remain recognisably observed; use broken ink and dry brush only at local edges rather than turning every surface into noise.
- Keep selected partial material cues: a glass reflection, a tile rhythm, a grouped noodle cluster, a botanical sprig, a fabric seam, a tray edge, or a leaf rhythm. Never render every surface or micro-detail, but never remove all the subject's character either.

## Composition

Keep the class coverage and generous paper field. The study should have a clear, readable silhouette and calm interior structure, while a few selected outer edges dissolve into paper. Do not reduce the subject into flat anonymous icons or an ultra-minimal poster; retain enough specific structure to feel observed. Do not fill the page with a detailed painting or create a patchwork of small fragmented colour blocks.

## Supporting marks

Use 2–5 small contextual marks sampled from visible source colour and geometry. Bars, dots, short strokes, and swatches are welcome when they echo the source; vary them by image and keep them subordinate.
