# Relaxed Editorial Print Direction

Use this direction for landscapes and architecture when the desired result is a loose archival print rather than a literal painted copy of a photograph.

## Translation rule

Retain the source's recognisable spatial relationships, but translate only its essential planes into five to ten uneven printed fragments. Prefer broad dry-brush sweeps, scumbled ink, hand-rolled colour fields, and deliberate omissions over continuously modelled objects, realistic lighting, or material detail.

## Perimeter rule

The scene must not have a clean enclosing outline. Let its perimeter break into interrupted marks, tapered brush ends, local overlaps, and dissolving paper gaps. A few source-led extensions can travel outward, while other portions disappear into the off-white paper.

## Source-linked companion marks

Add two to five quiet companion marks only when they can be inferred from visible source content. They must use the source palette and remain secondary to the study.

| Visible source cue | Companion mark |
| --- | --- |
| Mountain or ridge | Small contour/elevation line fragment |
| Lake, sea, or river | Thin wave/ripple sequence |
| Cloud or open sky | Broken sky-colour swatch or tiny bird glyphs when natural to the scene |
| Flower, grass, or tree | Botanical stem, grass stroke, or leaf mark |
| Chair, table, railing, bridge | Sparse structural line or plane motif |
| Vessel | Small contour mark |

Do not use a universal set of circles, grids, dots, or lines. If no meaningful source cue exists, leave the surrounding paper quiet.
