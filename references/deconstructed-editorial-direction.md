# Deconstructed Editorial Direction

Apply this direction to every class. The result is a designed reduction of the source, not a scaled-down realistic illustration.

## Three rules

1. **Reduce before rendering.** Keep the most recognisable silhouettes, spatial relationships, and four to eight descriptive anchors. Establish three to six connected colour masses first, then restore six to twelve selective source-derived details: a rim, overlap, seam, clustered ingredient division, branch rhythm, ridge break, structural joint, or similar identity cue. Preserve partial material cues while omitting photographic lighting, micro-detail, continuous modelling, and scattered micro-fragmentation. Do not reduce the scene to an ultra-minimal set of anonymous flat icons.
2. **Let paper breathe.** Place the complete study within its class coverage rule. The off-white paper must be a compositional field, not a uniform frame. Keep the study away from page edges and distribute empty space around it.
3. **Use a living perimeter, not noisy interiors.** Preserve readable, calm colour planes and selected internal structure. Break only chosen outer contours into one or two dry-brush ends, scumbled transitions, and occasional paper gaps. Never spread tiny holes, speckles, or disconnected chips throughout the subject.

## Source-adaptive supporting marks

Add two to five quiet supporting marks only when they arise from the visible source: an ingredient rhythm, a utensil stroke, a garment seam, a product profile, a cloud fragment, a ridge contour, or an architectural grid. They may become small colour chips, short bars, dots, or lines only when their colour and geometry are sampled from those source cues. Vary their placement; never apply a fixed decorative template.
