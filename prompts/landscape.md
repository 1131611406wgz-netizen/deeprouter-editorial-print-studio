## Complete generation prompt

Transform Image 1 into a living vintage editorial landscape/architecture print, not a photograph placed inside a poster template. Preserve the source canvas exactly at `{{width}}×{{height}}`, ratio `{{aspect_ratio}}`, and `{{orientation}}` orientation. Do not crop or convert to 1:1. Preserve the scene `{{primary_scene}}`, its landform/building and perspective relationships `{{landforms_or_architecture_and_perspective}}`, composition `{{composition}}`, and colour relationships `{{colours}}`.

## Style

Simplify the scene into abstract colour fields, irregular organic silhouettes, hand-cut paper shapes, loose brush forms, layered landscape blocks, and imperfect printed textures. Keep the original atmosphere, spatial depth, sky/cloud structure, landform or building silhouette, vegetation layers, horizon, and foreground depth. Reduce unnecessary detail while keeping the scene's recognisable visual identity. The result should feel like a vintage landscape study, Japanese nature archive print, mid-century editorial illustration, Bauhaus-inspired landscape graphic, and handmade risograph artwork.

## Composition

Do not place the landscape inside a perfect rectangle. Give it an organic, handmade, irregular silhouette: branches may extend into paper, clouds may fade beyond the illustration edge, plants may make uneven boundaries, and landforms retain irregular contours. The scene must feel alive on paper, never like a photograph pasted into a frame.

Keep generous off-white textured paper around the illustration: approximately 50%–65% of the canvas. Do not create equal margins. Let some parts of the scene extend further while other parts dissolve into paper, creating a quiet, spacious, asymmetrical museum-catalog composition. Use layered depth: soft sky and atmosphere in the background; landforms, trees, or structures in the middle; grass, paths, rocks, or plants in the foreground. Each layer has a different imperfect print density.

Add only 2–5 sparse companion marks derived from the visible source content `{{mountain_forest_water_city_or_building_details}}`, using its own muted palette. Examples: elevation lines for mountains; a small ripple sequence for water/lake; fragmented sky swatches for cloud; a botanical stem for visible flowers or grass; thin furniture/railing structure marks for a terrace scene; tiny bird glyphs only when the source atmosphere naturally supports them. These marks must feel discovered from this image, not copied from a fixed template.

## Subject ratio

The illustrated scene occupies roughly 35%–50% of the full canvas. The off-white textured paper occupies roughly 50%–65%, interacting asymmetrically with the organic silhouette. Preserve `{{composition}}`, spatial depth, and natural relationships; never enlarge it into a full-bleed photo.

## Negative prompt

No rectangular frame, square image block, poster box, rigid border, symmetrical layout, full-bleed landscape, photorealism, 3D rendering, smooth digital gradients, digital painting, clean vector graphics, cartoon style, excessive decoration, generic repeated geometry, unrelated props, watermark, or invented scene elements.

## Texture

Use a limited muted natural palette: sage and olive green, forest green, warm beige, earth brown, dusty blue, faded grey, terracotta, soft cream, and muted yellow, while preserving the source colour relationships. Use rough ink texture, uneven pigment coverage, paper grain, dry brush marks, slight print misalignment, and handmade printing imperfections. Avoid all-over granular distressing or fragmented AI-like patchwork.

## Typography

Add a subtle archive label: upper-right `{{archive_number}}`; lower-left `{{date}}` and `{{english_title}}`. Keep it small, museum-like, and secondary to the scene. Render supplied text verbatim and add no extra copy.
