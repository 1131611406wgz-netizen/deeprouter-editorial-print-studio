# Food / Beverage Editorial Print Prompt

## Input contract

- `source_size`: `{{width}}×{{height}}`, `{{orientation}}`, ratio `{{aspect_ratio}}`
- `subject`: `{{food_or_beverage}}`
- `container_and_arrangement`: `{{vessel_shape_and_relationships}}`
- `composition_to_preserve`: `{{composition}}`
- `colour_relationships`: `{{colours}}`
- `visible_decorators`: `{{ingredient_or_steam_or_utensil_details}}`
- `archive_number`, `date`, `english_title`: `{{archive_number}}`, `{{date}}`, `{{english_title}}`

## Complete generation prompt

Transform Image 1 into a living vintage editorial food-and-beverage print illustration, not a photo filter and not an ecommerce product poster. Preserve the source canvas exactly at `{{width}}×{{height}}`, ratio `{{aspect_ratio}}`, and `{{orientation}}` orientation. Do not crop or convert to 1:1. Preserve `{{food_or_beverage}}`, the vessel/arrangement relationship `{{vessel_shape_and_relationships}}`, the composition `{{composition}}`, and the colour relationships `{{colours}}`.

## Style

Simplify the food into abstract geometric shapes, irregular colour blocks, curved lines, organic silhouettes, and loose hand-printed forms. Preserve the main food, overall composition, dominant ingredients, container shape, and colour relationships, while reducing complexity. The result should resemble a vintage screen print, mid-century editorial illustration, Japanese food study, Bauhaus-inspired food graphic, and handmade risograph print. Do not render realistic ingredients, photographic lighting, gloss, or micro-detail.

## Composition

Place the food illustration small and centred on an off-white textured paper canvas. The illustration occupies only about 35%–45% of the total canvas; leave at least 55%–65% as uninterrupted paper. Keep generous margins on all four sides. Scale the subject down rather than enlarging it; do not zoom in, crop it, or let it touch page edges. It should feel quiet, spacious, balanced, and gallery-like—not an ecommerce pack shot.

Around the study, add only 2–5 quiet source-adaptive accents sampled from `{{ingredient_or_steam_or_utensil_details}}`: for example, a noodle curve, ingredient rhythm, utensil stroke, or colour-chip/bar/dot derived from the food palette and geometry. They must never compete with the food or become a repeated template.

## Subject ratio

The complete primary food/beverage and container group occupies about 35%–45% of the full canvas. Leave 55%–65% as uninterrupted off-white textured paper. Keep it fully visible, small, and away from every page edge.

## Negative prompt

No oversized food, close-up framing, tight crop, edge-to-edge layout, full-bleed ecommerce hero composition, poster-filling graphic, busy or dense composition, individually countable ingredients, repetitive peanut/seed/noodle marks, fully rendered food detail, glossy broth, realistic noodle texture, photorealism, glossy surfaces, smooth digital gradients, 3D, clean vector finish, cartoon outline, excessive decorative elements, unrelated ingredients or props, watermark, or rectangular photo pasted on paper.

## Texture

Warm beige, terracotta red, mustard yellow, olive/forest green, dusty orange, dark navy, earthy brown, soft grey, and faded cream; adapt them to preserve the source colour relationships. Add rough ink texture, uneven pigment coverage, subtle paper grain, broad dry-brush/brayer sweeps, local worn edges and ink skips, slight print misregistration, and imperfect handmade printing artifacts. Keep these tactile effects local and legible, never noisy or photo-like.

## Typography

Add a small subordinate museum-label treatment: upper-right `{{archive_number}}`; lower-left `{{date}}` and `{{english_title}}`. Render this text verbatim, keep it small, and add no other copy.
